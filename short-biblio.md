# Short Biography — GLG Research

Pablo Arrighi is a Senior Researcher (Research Director) at Inria and head of the QuaCS team (quantum computation structures), with a PhD from the University of Cambridge. He has 20+ years of research in quantum computing and quantum simulation, with 70+ peer-reviewed publications.

His technical expertise spans quantum programming and compilation (pioneer in quantum programming languages since 2003; CIFRE thesis co-supervised with Quandela on photonic quantum computing, 2022–2025), quantum simulation on near-term devices (quantum cellular automata and quantum walks), and quantum protocols and security (inventor of blind quantum computing — secure delegated quantum computation, now an active experimental subfield). He has written a systematic typology of quantum algorithms and popular science articles in La Recherche and Physics World.

On the evaluation side, he served on CoNRS Section 6 — the national committee responsible for selecting and promoting CNRS researchers in computer science (2019–2021) — and has refereed research proposals for funding agencies in Switzerland, Korea, Belgium, Luxembourg, Chile, Slovenia, Italy, Uruguay, and EU programmes. He is an HCERES expert (laboratory and university strategy evaluation) and a Crédit Impôt Recherche expert (private-sector R&D qualification). He is a member of the executive committee of the Quantum Saclay Center — which coordinates quantum activities across the Saclay plateau including startups such as Quandela and Pasqal — and of the Scientific and Training Advisory Board of the Quanta Institute at Aix-Marseille Université.

He advises independently through Quantum Information Expertise and works in French, English, and Spanish.

# Short Biography — Substack

I am a quantum computing researcher at Inria (France's national research institute for digital science), where I have spent 20 years studying how quantum mechanics changes what computers can do — and how fast they can do it.

I have worked on quantum programming languages, quantum simulation of physical systems, and quantum protocols (I introduced blind quantum computing, which lets you delegate a computation to an untrusted quantum server without revealing your data). I also wrote a typology of all known quantum algorithms, trying to make sense of the whole landscape.

Alongside my research, I evaluate quantum technology for investors and boards — which means I spend a lot of time reading claims made by startups and asking: is this real? Is it close? Does it matter?

This newsletter is where I think out loud about quantum computing and the industry forming around it — for readers who want more than hype, and less than a PhD.
